What is Agentic AI?

How Definitions Evolved

2023Abstract definitions. An agent is described by how it works: it senses its environment, makes decisions and acts, pursuing goals with limited supervision.

“AI systems that can pursue complex goals with limited direct supervision.”

Shavit et al. 2023

“AI agents are artificial entities that sense their environment, make decisions, and take actions.”

Xi et al. 2023

2024More concrete. Definitions name the mechanics: observing the world, acting through tools, and the model itself directing its own process and tool use.

“…an application that attempts to achieve a goal by observing the world and acting upon it using the tools that it has at its disposal.”

Wiesinger et al. 2024

Agents are systems where “LLMs dynamically direct their own processes and tool usage, maintaining control over how they accomplish tasks.”

Anthropic 2024

2025Independence and multiplicity. Agents accomplish tasks on their own, and agentic AI becomes teams of multiple agents collaborating toward complex goals.

“Agents are systems that independently accomplish tasks on your behalf.”

OpenAI 2025

“Systems of multiple AI agents collaborating to achieve complex goals.”

Sapkota et al. 2025

2026Scaling up. Agents decompose big problems into sub-tasks, delegate them to sub-agents and humans, adapt as conditions change, and act with direct effects on the systems they connect to.

AI agents need to meaningfully decompose problems into manageable sub-components, “and safely delegate their completion across to other AI agents and humans alike.”

Tomašev et al. 2026

AI agents “differ from earlier generative AI models in one important way: they can act, with direct effects on their environment and on the systems they connect to.”

Singapore Consensus 2026

What Makes an Agent

Characteristics what an agent does

Autonomy

Operates without direct human intervention and controls its own actions: the LLM directs its own process and tool use.

Wooldridge & Jennings 1995 · Anthropic 2024

Goal-directed

Takes the initiative toward a goal instead of only reacting, and accomplishes the task on the user's behalf.

Wooldridge & Jennings 1995 · OpenAI 2025

Perceives its environment

Senses the environment and responds to changes; each step brings ground truth, such as tool results or code output.

Wooldridge & Jennings 1995 · Anthropic 2024

Acts through tools

Uses tools to gather context and to take actions, choosing the right one for the current state of the workflow.

OpenAI 2025 · Schick et al. 2023

Reasons and plans

Interleaves reasoning with acting, breaks tasks into steps, reflects on past actions and corrects course.

Yao et al. 2022 · Weng 2023

Social ability

Interacts with other agents and humans: delegates, collaborates, hands off, and returns control when it cannot finish.

Wooldridge & Jennings 1995 · Tomašev et al. 2026

Building blocks what an agent is made of

An agent is an LLM that reasons and decides, guided by instructions and memory, acting on its environment and on other agents through tools, all inside guardrails.

Workflows and Agents

Workflows LLMs and tools orchestrated through predefined code paths

Prompt chaining

A sequence of steps; each LLM call processes the previous output.

Use when: the task splits into fixed subtasks

Routing

Classifies an input and sends it to a specialized follow-up task.

Use when: there are distinct categories to handle separately

Parallelization

LLMs work at the same time; outputs are aggregated by code (sectioning or voting).

Use when: you need speed or several perspectives

Orchestrator-workers

A central LLM breaks the task down, delegates to workers and synthesizes.

Use when: subtasks can't be predicted in advance

Evaluator-optimizer

One LLM generates, another evaluates, in a loop.

Use when: clear criteria make refinement pay off

OpenAI draws the same line: apps that use an LLM but don't let it control workflow execution, such as simple chatbots, single-turn LLMs or sentiment classifiers, are not agents.

Agents LLMs dynamically direct their own process and tool usage, alone or as a team

Single agent: the run loop

An LLM uses tools on feedback from the environment, in a loop, until an exit condition: a final-output tool, a reply with no tool call, an error or a turn limit.

Use when: steps can't be predicted. Start with one agent; add more only when it struggles with complex logic or too many similar tools.

Manager: agents as tools

A central agent coordinates specialized agents through tool calls and keeps control of the workflow and the user.

Use when: one agent should own the workflow and the conversation.

Decentralized: handoffs

Peer agents pass execution to one another. A handoff is a one-way transfer of control and conversation state.

Use when: no single agent needs central control; specialists can take over the user.

Guardrails and human oversight

Layered checks run alongside the agent: relevance and safety classifiers, PII filters, moderation, rules and risk ratings on tools.

Use when: always. Escalate to a human after repeated failures or before high-risk actions such as payments.
