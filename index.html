What is Agentic AI?

How Definitions Evolved

2023Abstract definitions. An agent is described by how it works: it senses its environment, makes decisions and acts, pursuing goals with limited supervision.

“AI systems that can pursue complex goals with limited direct supervision.”

Shavit et al. 2023

“AI agents are artificial entities that sense their environment, make decisions, and take actions.”

Xi et al. 2023

2024More concrete. Definitions name the mechanics: observing the world, acting through tools, and the model itself directing its own process and tool use.

“…an application that attempts to achieve a goal by observing the world and acting upon it using the tools that it has at its disposal.”

Wiesinger et al. 2024

Agents are systems where “LLMs dynamically direct their own processes and tool usage, maintaining control over how they accomplish tasks.”

Anthropic 2024

2025Independence and multiplicity. Agents accomplish tasks on their own, and agentic AI becomes teams of multiple agents collaborating toward complex goals.

“Agents are systems that independently accomplish tasks on your behalf.”

OpenAI 2025

“Systems of multiple AI agents collaborating to achieve complex goals.”

Sapkota et al. 2025

2026Scaling up. Agents decompose big problems into sub-tasks, delegate them to sub-agents and humans, adapt as conditions change, and act with direct effects on the systems they connect to.

AI agents need to meaningfully decompose problems into manageable sub-components, “and safely delegate their completion across to other AI agents and humans alike.”

Tomašev et al. 2026

AI agents “differ from earlier generative AI models in one important way: they can act, with direct effects on their environment and on the systems they connect to.”

Singapore Consensus 2026

Building Blocks, Workflows and Agents

Building block augmented LLM

An LLM enhanced with retrieval, tools and memory

The model actively directs these augmentations: it generates its own search queries, picks tools and decides what to remember.

Workflows LLMs and tools orchestrated through predefined code paths

Prompt chaining

A sequence of steps; each LLM call processes the previous output.

Use when: the task splits into fixed subtasks

Routing

Classifies an input and sends it to a specialized follow-up task.

Use when: there are distinct categories to handle separately

Parallelization

LLMs work at the same time; outputs are aggregated by code (sectioning or voting).

Use when: you need speed or several perspectives

Orchestrator-workers

A central LLM breaks the task down, delegates to workers and synthesizes.

Use when: subtasks can't be predicted in advance

Evaluator-optimizer

One LLM generates, another evaluates, in a loop.

Use when: clear criteria make refinement pay off

Agents LLMs dynamically direct their own process and tool usage

An LLM using tools based on feedback from the environment, in a loop

At each step it gets ground truth from the environment (tool results, code execution), can pause for human feedback, and stops on a condition or limit.

Use when: the problem is open-ended and the number of steps can't be predicted.

Source: Anthropic (2024). Building effective agents.
